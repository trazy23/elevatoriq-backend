Where Hospital Accounts Get Overcharged

⚠ HIGH — Callback Labor Billed Outside Contract Scope

Full-maintenance contracts cover callbacks during normal hours. Otis commonly reclassifies callbacks as "out of scope" by citing operator error, vandalism, or "misuse" — often loosely defined. In a high-traffic hospital, this can represent $10,000–$30,000+ in annual overbilling.

⚠ HIGH — Uncapped Annual Escalation

Otis contracts typically include 3–5% annual escalation, often tied to CPI or internal indices. Over 5–7 years without renegotiation, a contract at $1,000/unit can compound to $1,250–$1,400/unit. Many hospital FDs don't track the baseline — they just pay the invoice.

⚠ HIGH — Proprietary Parts Markups

Otis uses proprietary controllers on newer installs (Gen2, CompassPlus). Parts are billed at 40–80% above OEM cost. On a mixed-vintage fleet, older units may have non-proprietary components available at market rates — but owners are billed at proprietary pricing regardless.

⚠ HIGH — Construction Period Exposure (Context-Specific)

During the active $48M ED expansion, construction traffic increases elevator duty cycles on key banks. Otis will bill wear-related callbacks and component replacements attributable to construction traffic as out-of-scope. Without a pre-construction baseline, there's no documented position to dispute those charges.

▲ MEDIUM — Over-Contracted Units

A mixed-vintage 30-unit fleet almost certainly includes elevators that don't need full-maintenance coverage. Older hydraulic units in lower-traffic service roles, MOB elevators, and back-of-house freight lifts may be adequately served by oil & grease or parts & labor contracts at 30–50% lower cost per unit.
