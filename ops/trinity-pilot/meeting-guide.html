2–12 min
Discovery — The Most Important Part
Listen more than you talk. Take notes.

Ask these five questions. Let him answer fully before moving on. The information you gather here shapes everything that follows.

Question 1 — Contract History
"How long have you been with the Otis contract here, and when did you last go out to bid on maintenance?"
Listen for: How long they've been locked in, whether they've ever tested the market, how much leverage they believe they have. If they haven't bid in 5+ years, that's a strong signal that they have no benchmark to compare against — and almost certainly don't know what competitive pricing looks like today.
Question 2 — Invoice Review Process
"When you get an Otis invoice, what's your process for reviewing it — do you have a way to verify what's being billed versus what's actually covered under the contract?"
Listen for: The core pain point. Most FDs will admit — at some level — that they don't have a system for this. If Rocky says "we just approve it," that's your opening. If he says he does review it, ask what that looks like — you'll find the gaps in the process quickly.
Question 3 — Contract Structure Across the Fleet
"With a mixed-vintage fleet across the campus, are all 30 units on the same contract structure, or do you have different tiers by equipment age or type?"
Listen for: Over-contracting. If all 30 units are on the same full-maintenance tier regardless of age, use, or duty cycle, that's significant opportunity. Older hydraulic units in the MOB or back-of-house likely don't need full-maintenance coverage at hospital rates.
Question 4 — Surprise Charges
"Have you had any surprise repair bills or callback charges that felt out of scope? How did you handle those?"
Listen for: A specific story. If yes — that's your demo use case. That's exactly what Module A surfaces. Bonus context: with the $48M ED expansion underway, construction-related callback charges are almost certain to show up. If they already have, that's your lead finding before you even run the analysis.
Question 5 — Modernization Horizon
"What does modernization look like for this portfolio — do you have units approaching end of useful life, and how are you evaluating those decisions?"
Listen for: Opens the Module B conversation (single modernization or multi-bid comparison). Also tells you whether this is a short-term contract management conversation or a longer-term capital planning relationship. Hospital elevators typically have a 25–30 year useful life; a mixed-vintage fleet almost certainly has units approaching that threshold.
